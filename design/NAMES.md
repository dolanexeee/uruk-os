# URUK OS — Identity Names

Proposed renames so every app carries the Mesopotamian / URUK identity instead of echoing macOS.
Rule applied throughout: **short, pronounceable, and tied to function** — the name teaches what the app does, the icon confirms it.

| Current name | New name | Arabic | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finder | **Uruk Files** | ملفات أوروك | The file manager is the front door of the system, so it carries the master brand itself — every user learns "Uruk" from the app they open most. |
| Terminal | **Uruk Shell** | صدفة أوروك | Honest and instantly understood by anyone technical; "Uruk" stamps ownership without hiding what it is. |
| Editor | **Scribe** | المِرقَم | The scribe (mirqam = reed stylus) was Mesopotamia's writer and programmer in one — a text editor named for the first people who ever wrote text down. |
| Settings | **Ukkin** | أوكّين | The Ukkin was the Sumerian assembly where decrees were set for the city — exactly what a settings app is: where the system's decrees are decided. |
| Photos | **Shamash Gallery** | معرض شمش | Shamash is the sun god of light and sight; a gallery is a house of light and images. His disc is the icon. |
| Preview | **Shamash Eye** | عين شمش | Shamash sees everything the sun touches; the Eye is the app that looks at images and documents before anything else does. |
| Notes | **Tablet** | اللوح | Notes were invented here — pressed into clay tablets. The shortest possible name, and nobody who sees the icon will misunderstand it. |
| Calculator | **Abacus** | المعداد | Calculation began in Mesopotamia (sexagesimal numerals, counting boards). "Abacus" is understood worldwide and needs no explanation. |
| Calendar | **Nanna** | نانّا | Nanna, moon god of Ur, was the keeper of the calendar — months were counted by his phases. A calendar named for the original calendar-keeper. |
| Music | **Lyre** | القيثارة | The Lyre of Ur (~4500 years old, gold and lapis) is one of the oldest instruments on Earth. One word, unmistakable icon. |
| Browser | **Ishtar Gate** | بوابة عشتار | The browser is a gate you pass through to reach the world; Babylon's great gate, with Ishtar's star shining in the passage, is the most famous doorway of the ancient world. |
| Uruk Code | **Uruk Code** | كود أوروك | Name kept — it already matches the suite (Uruk Cut, Uruk OS) and developers read it instantly. Only its icon changes: brackets cut like wedges. |
| Launchpad | **Plaza** | الساحة | Every city of Uruk gathered in its plaza; this is the plaza where all the apps gather. Short, warm, and its job is obvious the moment it opens. |
| Trash | **Shard** | الشقفة | Broken pottery shards were the ancient world's refuse — and its scratch paper. What you throw away in Uruk OS lands in the shard pile. |
| Clock | **Clepsydra** | الساعة المائية | The water clock (Greek *klepsydra*, from Mesopotamian outflow vessels) is among the first timekeepers ever built. Pronounced klep-SY-dra. |
| Weather | **Adad** | أدد | Adad is the storm and weather god — rain, wind and flood were his domain. Two syllables, easy in Arabic and English alike. |
| Maps | **Two Rivers** | النهرين | Mesopotamia literally means "the land between the two rivers." A maps app named for the geography that named a civilization. |
| Tools | **Forge** | المشغل | Utilities are the workshop of the system; bronze was forged in Uruk's workshops before almost anywhere else. One strong word. |

## System glyphs (not apps, no rename needed)

| Glyph | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Generic folder | Plain gold folder, deliberately emblem-free — it is a working container, not an app. |
| Generic file | A cream sheet whose "text" is written in cuneiform wedges — even unknown files belong to the identity. |

## Conservative alternates (if any name feels too bold)

- Ishtar Gate → **Uruk Web** (plainer, follows the brief's example)
- Ukkin → **Uruk Settings** (clearest, least character)
- Clepsydra → **Water Clock** (says exactly what it is)
- Plaza → **Uruk Apps** (functional, forgettable)

These are fallbacks only; the primary table is the recommendation.
